1. Start with a research question

Stock Signals brings trade ideas, options activity, public disclosures and stock research together. It is a research app; it does not submit orders, connect to your brokerage to trade or manage a portfolio for you. Availability varies with app version, plan and data source.

  1. Find an idea: open Stocks or Options and read the available setup. Check whether it is active or closed.
  2. Add context: search for the ticker or company in Stocks and open its research screen to compare price action, news and analyst information.
  3. Compare activity: explore Live, Political and Insider, checking the transaction, filing and data timestamps.

Signals are reference information, not a personalised instruction. Read the financial disclaimer before relying on any display.

2. Find your way around

The main navigation contains Stocks, Options, Live, Political, Insider, Watchlist and Settings. Tap a destination to open it. Search and filter controls differ by screen. Use the screen’s reset control when you want to clear filters; a narrow search or date range can hide otherwise available records.

Settings contains notification preferences, Help Guide, Disclaimer, Terms of Service, Privacy Policy and Customer Support. In the current native app, the help and legal documents are bundled for offline reading; these website pages are separate copies. Interface wording can vary by app version and device.

3. Read stock signals

Open Stocks. Switch between Stock Signals and Closed Signals to review available current ideas and historical records. Search by ticker or company name and select a result to open its research screen. A locked symbol or premium control indicates that the relevant access is restricted by your plan.

  • Buy target / entry: the setup’s reference entry price, not an order placed for you.
  • Sell target: a reference exit level, not a guaranteed future price.
  • Stop loss: a downside reference where supplied, not a guaranteed maximum loss.
  • Potential gain: the stated upside scenario. For a simple long-stock example, (target − entry) ÷ entry × 100 calculates the percentage before costs.
  • Reported / total gain: a signal-reference figure. It is not your account’s return and may differ materially after execution, timing and costs.

Example only: a $100 entry reference and $120 target imply 20% upside if that target is reached. This arithmetic is not a forecast or a recommendation to trade.

4. Understand an option signal

Open Options, search by symbol or company where available, and switch between active and closed signals. Tap a row to expand its contract details. Read the strategy, call/put side, strike and expiry together, then compare premium, target, stop loss, risk and sentiment fields where supplied.

A standard US equity option commonly represents 100 shares. A quoted $2.00 premium commonly means $200 for one standard contract before fees; adjusted contracts can have a different deliverable or multiplier. Confirm the exact contract terms with your broker. A stock price, option premium and flow value use different units and must not be substituted for one another.

Options can expire worthless; some strategies can lose more than the initial amount invested. A probability or potential-gain field is an estimate, not a promise. The Options Industry Council’s options basics is an external educational reference, not a substitute for your broker’s contract information.

5. Explore Live Flow

Open Live, search for a ticker, and expand a contract row to inspect available strike, expiry, last price, volume, open interest (OI), estimated premium, volume/OI, days to expiry (DTE) and implied volatility (IV). The screen refreshes periodically while open; that does not guarantee exchange-real-time data.

Use the filter control to narrow estimated premium, volume, OI, DTE and call/put side. Tap Apply to use the choices or Reset to clear them. The flow-bias summary changes with the rows selected by your filters.

Estimated flow premium is based on contract price × displayed volume × 100 in this view. A $2 price × 500 contracts × 100 illustrates $100,000 of estimated value, not profit, one verified order or proof of a new position. Whale, burst, spike, fresh, weekly and bullish/bearish labels are screening descriptions. Calls or puts may be part of a hedge, a closing trade or a multi-leg strategy.

6. Research Political Flow

Open Political to explore disclosed politician activity. Available fields include the person, symbol or asset description, transaction type, reported amount, owner, transaction date and disclosure date. Amounts are often ranges rather than an exact execution value.

Search by symbol or politician. Use filters for politician, transaction type, minimum reported value, asset type and owner. Choose a date range and whether it applies to transaction date or disclosure date. Available ordering includes newest transaction, newest disclosure and largest reported value. Apply choices or reset to the default view.

Read both dates: a trade on 2 October disclosed on 9 October describes activity from 2 October. A new disclosure is not necessarily a new trade. A reported holder can be a spouse or another owner; do not assume every record is the politician’s personal purchase. Free access may show a labeled demo; the live-access feed requires the relevant entitlement.

7. Research Insider Flow

Open Insider to browse reported corporate insider activity. Rows show available ticker, name, role, transaction type, shares, transaction price/value, trade date and filing date. Search by symbol, company or insider. Pull down to refresh and scroll to request more available history. Previously loaded records may be cached, so check freshness.

Use Filters to select transaction types such as purchases, sales, exercises, awards, gifts, tax withholding and conversions; narrow by value and reported role, including CEO, CFO, officer, director or significant owner. You can select a transaction-date range and sort by filing date, transaction date or value.

Tap a transaction to open its SEC filing where available. Read its notes and other transactions before interpreting it. An award, exercise or tax-related transaction is different from an open-market purchase. Free access may show a labeled demo, while access to the live feed requires the relevant entitlement. Here “live” access means the available updated disclosure feed, not instant observation of the underlying trade.

8. Use charts and stock research

Search in Stocks by ticker or company name, or select a stock from a discovery list, to open research. The quote, daily change and high/low are shown where available. Check any delayed-price label; different research fields may have different source dates.

  • Chart: choose Candles or Line. Available bar intervals include 5-minute, 30-minute, daily, weekly and monthly; intraday access is subject to Pro restrictions. The loaded history depends on the interval.
  • MA 20: toggles the average closing price over the last 20 valid bars.
  • Inspect and zoom: hold the chart to inspect a bar’s date, open, high, low, close and volume. Pinch to zoom; use the circular reset arrow to restore the view. Chart times are shown in New York time.
  • Range percentage: compares the first and last closing prices in the loaded history. It is not a signal return or your portfolio performance.
  • News: select a headline to open its external source.
  • Technicals: available fields include the 50-day average, 52-week range, price-to-book, recommendation and analyst target range.
  • Analysis: reviews analyst recommendation categories, including strong buy, buy, hold, sell and strong sell. Those views can change and are not instructions to you.

Missing, delayed or inconsistent data should be checked against a reliable source. The app does not guarantee a quote or target is executable.

9. Browse Watchlist and stock-discovery lists

Watchlist contains curated screening lists such as Most Active, Day Gainers, Day Losers, Undervalued Large Caps, Aggressive Small Caps, Small Cap Gainers, Growth Technology Stocks, Most Shorted Stocks, Undervalued Growth Stocks and Top ETFs.

Open a list, then select a stock to view research. These are discovery lists, not an imported portfolio, a record of your brokerage orders or a promise that the listed stocks are suitable for you. Do not assume this tab tracks your actual balance or realised profit.

10. Configure notifications

Open Settings and enable push notifications. Allow notification permission in iOS Settings as well. Choose stock signals, options signals, politician flow and insider flow alerts as available. Political and insider preferences include minimum transaction values in USD; insider preferences also include purchase and sale switches. Preferences save automatically, but check the displayed sync status if a change is waiting for connectivity.

Allowing notifications does not unlock premium content. Some preferences or feeds can depend on your plan. If alerts do not arrive, check the master switch, feed switch, thresholds, iOS permission, Focus, Scheduled Summary and your connection. A narrowly filtered feed may have no matching new records.

Alerts can be late, duplicated or missed. A filing alert may concern an older trade; a reminder may simply prompt you to review the app. Opening an alert does not place an order. You can disable notifications in the app and control lock-screen previews in iOS Settings. See the Privacy Policy for registration data and choices.

The app is free to download, with premium access available through eligible App Store products. 12. Troubleshoot an empty or stale screen

No matching records: clear search, reset filters, widen the date range and confirm whether the range uses trade date or filing date. In Live Flow, try a more active symbol; traded contracts do not necessarily meet unusual-activity criteria. An empty result is different from a network error.

Loading error or old data: check the connection, use refresh or retry where offered, and inspect timestamps. Cached records are not confirmation that the feed has just updated. Quotes, research fields and filings have different schedules; newly filed records can concern older transactions. If the source is unavailable, retrying does not guarantee fresh data.

Controls differ from this guide: check your app version, device and plan. Some screens vary between iPhone and iPad. To report an issue, include the screen, ticker, filters, app version, approximate time and a screenshot.

13. A short glossary

Call / put: a call gives the holder a contractual right to buy the underlying; a put gives a right to sell it, on the contract’s terms. A strategy can contain multiple legs.

Strike / expiry / DTE: strike is the contract’s exercise price; expiry is its expiration date; DTE means days to expiry. Expiry is not necessarily the day you intend to trade.

Volume / open interest: volume counts contracts traded during a reported period; OI counts outstanding contracts and can update on a different schedule. High volume relative to OI does not prove all trades opened new positions.

IV: implied volatility reflects volatility implied by option prices. It is not a forecast of direction.

Exercise / assignment: exercise uses the option holder’s contractual right; assignment requires the option seller to fulfil the exercised contract. Timing, deliverables and settlement depend on the contract and broker.

Transaction / filing date: the first describes when reported activity occurred; the second when it was disclosed or filed. Sort and compare accordingly.

Insider transaction codes: common codes include P (purchase), S (sale), M (exercise or conversion of a derivative security received from the company), A (grant or award), G (gift), F (payment of exercise price or tax liability using securities) and C (conversion of a derivative security). Read the exact filing, code definition and notes; a short display label can omit detail. See the SEC’s guide to insider transaction reports.

Analyst target / sentiment: these describe a source’s view or model summary, not a confirmed outcome. A mean target is an average; a median is the middle observation. The horizon can differ from the signal you are reviewing.

14. Get support and review the legal documents

In the app, open Settings → Customer Support, or use our support form or manimau@outlook.com. For a questionable disclosure, include the filing link and relevant dates. Send only the information needed to investigate; never include passwords, registration secrets, full payment-card numbers or brokerage credentials.

Support cannot place trades, manage your account, promise investment outcomes or make a personalised recommendation about your portfolio. For those decisions, consult your broker or a suitably licensed adviser.
